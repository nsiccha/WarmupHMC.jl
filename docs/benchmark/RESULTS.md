# Measured: adaptive reparametrization vs the fixed centering endpoints

Machine-generated tables come from `results/runs.json` via `summarize.jl`;
`README.md` says how to reproduce the whole thing. 8 pinned seeds per arm,
`n_draws` floor 1000, Julia 1.10.11, single-threaded BLAS, 184 runs, 0 failed.

## Verdict

**Adaptive partial centering, started from the centered parametrization, beats
the plain sampler on every target measured — and beats the hand-written
noncentered model on four of five.** Per gradient evaluation it is 2.6× to 134×
better than plain. It reaches this without being told which parametrization to
use.

**It is not free, and on two targets it is a net wall-clock loss.** The
`ReparametrizedProblem` wrapper costs ×5.2–×20.4 per `logdensity_and_gradient`
call. Where the parametrization pathology is severe (eight_schools, the funnel)
the sampling gain dwarfs that and adaptive wins by 51×–79× in ESS/second. Where
the pathology is mild (both radon models, seeds) the gain per gradient is real
but smaller than the per-call cost, and **adaptive is 1.3×–4.2× slower in
wall-clock than doing nothing at all.**

So the honest one-line claim the package can make today is about *gradient
evaluations*, not seconds:

> Starting from a centered parametrization, adaptive partial centering reaches
> or exceeds hand-written-noncentered sampling efficiency per gradient
> evaluation, without being told to. Whether that converts into wall-clock
> depends on how expensive the target's own gradient is relative to the
> wrapper.

Two findings sharpen this:

1. **The method is not merely auto-selecting an endpoint.** On eight_schools and
   the funnel it drives `c` to 0.0 — noncentered, the known right answer. But on
   both radon models it settles at an *interior* `c ≈ 0.4–0.5`, and on seeds at
   `c ≈ 0.2–0.3`, and at those interior values it beats **both** endpoints and
   posteriordb's hand-written noncentered model. That is a result no fixed
   parametrization can reach, and it is the strongest argument for the method.
2. **The overhead is not the transform.** A wrapper configured as an exact
   identity costs essentially the same as a live one (×5.33 vs ×5.42, ×11.35 vs
   ×10.88, ×20.23 vs ×20.43). The cost is carrying the AD re-differentiation of
   `ljac_(x_) + dot(g_y, y_)` at all, not the arithmetic of the reparametrization.
   That makes it an implementation cost rather than a property of the method,
   and it is where the wall-clock losses above would be recovered.

## What each arm is

Every arm samples the **same** Stan model, so all draws are in the same
coordinates and their ESS is directly comparable. The arms differ only in what
the sampler may do with the partial-centering parameter `c`
(`PartiallyCentered(1.0)` is centered, `(0.0)` is noncentered).

| arm | what it is |
|---|---|
| `plain` | bare `StanProblem`, no wrapper. What a user gets today. |
| `fixed c = centered` | wrapped, source `c` pinned to the model's own value, `nonlinear_adapt=false`. An exact identity transform, so it isolates the cost of merely carrying the wrapper. |
| `adaptive` | wrapped, source `c` starts at the model's own value, `nonlinear_adapt=true`. The method under test. |
| `fixed c = noncentered` | wrapped, source `c` pinned to the opposite endpoint. The hand-written noncentered parametrization, reached by transform. |
| `hand-written noncentered model` | posteriordb's separately written noncentered member, sampled plain. The external reference for "noncentered-like". |

`min ESS` is the minimum over coordinates — the number that governs how long you
must run. Sub-figures are the min–max across the 8 seeds. `final source c` is
read back off the mutated `IndexedReparametrization` after the run.

## Where it fails

**The adaptation does not always move.** On 1 of 8 funnel seeds and 1 of 8 seeds
on `seeds_data`, `c` finished at 1.0 — the centered value it started from —
meaning adaptation never escaped.

| target | stuck seed | its min ESS | median of the other 7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `funnel` | 7 | **16.8** | 769.6 |
| `seeds_data-seeds_centered_model` | 8 | 181.4 | 303.5 |

On `seeds_data` this is benign — the stuck seed still beats the plain median. On
the funnel it is a **45× collapse** to roughly plain-centered performance, with a
divergence. That tail is why the ranges are reported and why the medians above
should not be read as a guarantee. A one-in-eight chance of getting no benefit
at all is a real limitation, and it is the single most actionable defect these
measurements surface.

## Cost on the gradient hot path

`ReparametrizedProblem` re-differentiates `ljac_(x_) + dot(g_y, y_)` at every
`logdensity_and_gradient`. Nanoseconds per call, 2000 calls at random positions:

| target | bare | wrapped, no-op | wrapped, live | no-op cost | live cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `eight_schools-eight_schools_centered` | 535 | 2856 | 2903 | ×5.33 | ×5.42 |
| `radon_mn-radon_partially_pooled_centered` | 36997 | 419849 | 402437 | ×11.35 | ×10.88 |
| `radon_mn-radon_variable_intercept_centered` | 42161 | 225811 | 218045 | ×5.36 | ×5.17 |
| `seeds_data-seeds_centered_model` | 2733 | 20760 | 18758 | ×7.60 | ×6.86 |
| `funnel` | 46 | 926 | 935 | ×20.23 | ×20.43 |

**The no-op and live columns agree to within noise on every target.** An
identity reparametrization — source `c` equal to target `c`, so the transform
provably does nothing — pays the same ×5–×20 as a live one. The cost is the AD
machinery itself, not the reparametrization.

The two radon rows are also the two wall-clock losses above; the ×10.88 on
`radon_partially_pooled` is what turns a 2.8× per-gradient win into a 4.2×
wall-clock loss. Note the `plain` arm calls BridgeStan's own gradient with no
Julia AD in the loop at all, so this comparison is what a user actually
experiences, not a like-for-like AD comparison.

## Validation: the no-op arm really is a no-op

`plain` and `fixed c = centered` are mathematically the same sampler. They agree
**exactly** — identical gradient-evaluation counts and ESS on all 8 seeds — on
`seeds_data` and the funnel, and disagree on eight_schools and both radon
models.

That split is not noise, and its cause is identifiable: the two targets that
agree are exactly the two whose reparametrization has a **constant** location
(`Float64` for `seeds_data`, `0.0` for the funnel). Every target that disagrees
has a location that is a closure over the position vector, where reconstructing
`loc + exp(s)·((x − loc)/exp(s))` is the identity only up to floating-point
rounding — and NUTS amplifies a 1e-16 gradient difference into a different
trajectory. Where the arithmetic permits an exact identity, the wrapper delivers
one; where it does not, the two arms stay within seed noise of each other. That
is the harness checking itself.

## What this does not measure

- **One chain per run.** No R-hat, no between-chain diagnostics. `min ESS` is
  within-chain, computed from the draws by `MCMCDiagnosticTools`, not read from
  `result.ess` (which is all zeros unless `monitor_ess=true`).
- **No reference-posterior accuracy check.** These are efficiency measurements.
  Nothing here verifies the draws are from the right distribution — that is
  `WarmupHMC:reparam-verify`'s scope, and the two should be read together before
  any performance claim ships.
- **Wall-clock is machine-specific** (measured on `strato2`, single-threaded
  BLAS). ESS per gradient evaluation is the portable number.
- **Five targets.** Four posteriordb hierarchical models plus one synthetic
  funnel. Every posteriordb target with a ready spec in
  `web/src/posteriordb_reparametrizations.jl` and a non-empty reparametrization
  is included; posteriordb ships no funnel.
